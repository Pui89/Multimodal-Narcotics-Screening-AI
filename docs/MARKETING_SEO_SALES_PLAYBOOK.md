# Free-First Marketing, Trust and Partnership Playbook

## Purpose
Present the multimodal screening concept to potential technical, laboratory, safety, and public-sector reviewers in a careful, evidence-led way.

## Positioning
**A research and engineering concept for multimodal screening support with uncertainty reporting and human review.**

Important boundaries:
- Do not claim that a model, ordinary RGB image, or confidence score alone confirms a substance's chemical identity.
- Do not advertise validated narcotics identification, field reliability, or law-enforcement readiness without suitable independent evidence and lawful validation.
- Clearly distinguish implemented code, synthetic/demo inputs, research hypotheses, and future hardware.
- Avoid publishing operational details that could enable evasion or misuse.

## Free-first channel plan
1. **Public project page:** describe intended scope, modalities under study, limitations, privacy protections, and the review process.
2. **Safe demo:** use synthetic, public, or explicitly authorised non-sensitive examples. Show uncertainty and a human-review step. Never upload confidential case files to third-party demo tools.
3. **Search visibility:** use precise, non-sensational terms such as multimodal screening research, sensor-fusion evaluation, uncertainty estimation, and human-in-the-loop review.
4. **Evidence notes:** publish test protocols, dataset provenance, error analysis, and reproducible results when available. Explain false positives and false negatives rather than reporting accuracy alone.
5. **Expert feedback:** seek input from qualified analytical chemistry, laboratory, privacy, safety, and ML evaluation experts. Describe the system as a concept until it has passed the relevant validation.
6. **Responsible enquiries:** invite discussion about evaluation methodology, data governance, and safe research collaboration—not operational deployment.

## Suggested page structure
- Intended research question and scope.
- Modalities and data flow, without sensitive operational instructions.
- Uncertainty handling, out-of-distribution limitations, and human review.
- Dataset source, licence, privacy, and representativeness.
- Evaluation protocol and known failure modes.
- Current status, intended use, prohibited assumptions, and contact route.

## Trust and SEO checklist
- State what the system cannot determine and when it must abstain.
- Do not use fake endorsements, customer logos, law-enforcement claims, or fabricated test results.
- Avoid exposing personal, biometric, case, or other sensitive data.
- Use descriptive page titles and image alt text; label synthetic and illustrative visuals.
- A free public-page audit may help identify technical SEO issues, but it cannot validate scientific or legal claims.

## Partnership questions
1. What screening-support task is within the partner's lawful scope?
2. What independent reference method would establish ground truth?
3. Which false-positive and false-negative risks matter most?
4. What privacy, retention, access-control, and audit requirements apply?
5. What review and abstention behaviour must be demonstrated before any pilot?

## Free-first tools and cautions
- **FreeScan.app:** optional first-pass website audit; paid monitoring is not needed for a basic page check.
- **LiveDemo:** optional for a limited public walkthrough, but never upload sensitive data; a local synthetic-data demo is safer.
- **Ads, scraping, and automated DMs:** not recommended before the claims, audience, and lawful use are well-defined.
- Do not connect external data services or enable paid APIs/hosting without explicit approval.

## Definition of done
- Claims are bounded and supported by evidence.
- Demo data is safe to publish and has verified permission.
- Uncertainty, abstention, and human review are clearly explained.
- Privacy and misuse boundaries are visible.
- No paid service or external account is enabled without approval.
